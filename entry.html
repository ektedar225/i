AI & Computer Vision

Industrial Defect Detection System for Manufactured Parts

Institution IIT Jodhpur
Timeline April 2026
Key Stack PyTorch, OpenCV
Co-Authors Arush & Harshita

Overview & Motivation

Automated visual inspection constitutes the critical infrastructure of quality assurance within contemporary manufacturing environments. Traditional supervised deep learning approaches face a fundamental bottleneck: defective training samples are extremely scarce, and the morphological manifestation of a defect is highly unpredictable. It is logistically impossible to construct a supervised dataset encompassing all potential failure modes.

To resolve this profound data imbalance, this project presents an unsupervised anomaly detection system. Instead of teaching a network what every defect looks like, the model is trained exclusively on defect-free ("normal") images to learn the normative statistical distribution and the latent mathematical representation of perfection.

Core Architecture

The foundational neural architecture deployed is a U-Net Convolutional Autoencoder (UNetAE), which comprises:

  • Encoder Sub-Network: Systematically compresses the high-resolution input image into a significantly lower-dimensional latent space using a hierarchy of convolutional layers and spatial downsampling.
  • Decoder Sub-Network: Operates as the mathematical inverse of the encoder, utilizing bilinear upsampling to systematically reconstruct the original image from the compressed state while avoiding checkerboard artifacts.
  • U-Net Skip Connections: These connections directly bridge each encoder level with its symmetric decoder level. This preserves high-frequency spatial information that would otherwise be lost through the bottleneck, enabling the fine-grained spatial reconstructions necessary for pixel-level defect localization.

Loss Function & Optimization

Training employs a combined loss function of α-MSE + β-SSIM (α=0.6, β=0.4):

  • Mean Squared Error (MSE): Metric penalizes large pixel-level errors globally.
  • Structural Similarity Index (SSIM): Preserves local structure, luminance, and contrast patterns.
Together, they produce sharper, more faithful reconstructions than either metric alone. The system is optimized using the AdamW optimizer with a CosineAnnealingLR scheduler.

Anomaly Detection & Inference

During the inference phase, the model easily processes flawless parts, but struggles to process aberrant features (like scratches or dents) that exist outside its learned normative distribution.

  • Defective regions produce elevated per-pixel reconstruction errors.
  • This mathematical divergence is smoothed with a Gaussian filter, normalized, and projected back into spatial dimensions to create an anomaly heatmap.
  • By overlaying a JET colormap onto the original image, the system flags the entire image as anomalous while precisely localizing the exact spatial coordinates of the defect.

Evaluation & Results

The pipeline was rigorously evaluated on the MVTec Anomaly Detection benchmark, specifically utilizing the "leather" category. The system successfully bridges the gap between deep learning theory and industrial viability, achieving an image-level AUROC of 0.5710 and a pixel-level AUROC of 0.8318. This demonstrates strong, highly accurate spatial localization of defects without requiring a single annotated example of a failure state during training.

Process Control & RL

AI-Driven PID Controller Tuning for Nonlinear Systems

Course CHL4020: Process Control
Advisor Dr. Santosh K. Varanasi
Key Stack PyTorch, Gym, SciPy
Project Level L3 (Reproduction + Upgrade)

Overview & Motivation

PID controllers form the backbone of industrial process automation. However, designing and tuning these controllers for real-world chemical and biotechnological systems presents a significant challenge due to severe nonlinearities. The traditional engineering approach requires linearizing the system around a specific operating point. While functional, this method suffers from performance degradation the moment the system dynamics shift away from that calculated linearization point.

This project explores a modern, model-free alternative by successfully reproducing and substantially improving upon the research paper "Tuning of PID Controllers Using Reinforcement Learning for Nonlinear System Control" (Processes 2025). By leveraging the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) reinforcement learning algorithm, the system autonomously learns optimal PID gains ($K_p$, $K_i$, $K_d$) through direct interaction with the nonlinear plant, completely bypassing the need for explicit mathematical linearization.

Phase 1: Architecture & Baseline Reproduction

In this architecture, the PID controller is modeled mathematically as a single-layer linear neural network without an activation function. The three weights of this network directly correspond to the PID gains. The state fed into the RL agent consists of the exact signals a standard PID utilizes: the error ($e$), the integral of the error ($\int e\,dt$), and the derivative of the error ($de/dt$).

To validate the generalizability of the TD3 algorithm, I reconstructed the pipeline in PyTorch and deployed it across four distinct dynamical environments:

  • Continuous Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR): Governing chemical concentration control.
  • Biotechnological Reactor: Replicating the baseline paper's focus on bacterial growth and substrate concentration.
  • Nonlinear Water Tank: Managing fluid levels with nonlinear outflow dynamics.
  • Second-Order Mechanical System: Controlling position via force inputs.
The baseline TD3 agent successfully converged to stable control policies across all environments, matching the performance of classical tuning methods without requiring system equations.

Phase 2: Algorithmic Improvements & Addressing "Integrator Collapse"

During the reproduction phase, I identified a critical flaw in the original paper's LQG-style reward function formulation. The original reward penalized the absolute magnitude of the control effort ($u^2$). This inadvertently caused Integrator Collapse—the RL agent learned to drive the integral gain ($K_i$) to zero, accepting a permanent steady-state offset simply to minimize continuous control effort.

To resolve this and enhance the robustness of the agent, I engineered an "Improved TD3" architecture featuring three major algorithmic upgrades:

  • Δu Reward Formulation with Overshoot Penalty: I redesigned the reward function to penalize the rate of change of the control signal ($\Delta u$) rather than its absolute magnitude, and introduced a targeted penalty for setpoint overshoot ($e_{overshoot}^2$). Because maintaining a constant control signal at a steady state now costs the agent nothing, the integrator is free to eliminate the offset, completely resolving the $K_i \approx 0$ bug.
  • Prioritized Experience Replay (PER): Standard TD3 samples training data uniformly. I implemented PER to force the agent to prioritize learning from transitions with high TD-errors (i.e., highly "surprising" states), which accelerated learning and improved stability.
  • Curriculum Learning: To prevent the agent from getting trapped in poor regional minima during early training phases, I introduced a curriculum that starts with easier initial conditions (close to the setpoint) and gradually increases difficulty.

Results & Impact

The Improved TD3 model demonstrated vastly superior transient performance. In complex environments like the CSTR, the improved agent not only converged faster due to the PER implementation, but also maintained a perfect zero steady-state error thanks to the modified Δu reward logic. The explicit overshoot penalty ensured that mechanical and chemical systems reached their setpoints smoothly with minimal, safe oscillations. Ultimately, this project successfully bridges modern AI capabilities with fundamental chemical engineering process control, proving that RL is a highly practical, model-free alternative to classical PID tuning.

Chemical Plant Design

Industrial-Scale Plant Design for N-Butyl Acetate Production

Institution IIT Jodhpur
Course Process Plant Design
Simulation Software ASPEN Plus V14
Team Partners Hemant & Prasangeet

Project Overview

This project details the conceptual design and economic evaluation of a large-scale chemical plant capable of producing 1000 TPD (Tonnes Per Day) of N-Butyl Acetate. Developed as part of the Process Plant Design and Economics coursework at IIT Jodhpur, the simulation demonstrates the technical and economic viability of utilizing advanced distillation techniques for high-yield chemical synthesis.

Core Process & Chemistry

The core chemical pathway involves the esterification of acetic acid with 1-butanol:

Acetic Acid + 1-Butanol ⇔ N-Butyl Acetate + Water

To overcome the thermodynamic equilibrium limitations typical of standard esterification reactions, the process strategically incorporates toluene as an entrainer. This entrainer-assisted reactive distillation actively removes the water byproduct via a heterogeneous azeotrope, driving the reaction forward continuously to maximize both the yield and purity of the N-Butyl Acetate product.

Simulation & Equipment Specifications

The entire process flowsheet was rigorously modeled and simulated from the ground up using ASPEN Plus V14. Key unit operations and equipment specifications were explicitly designed and sized for industrial-scale robustness:

  • Primary Reactor (R-101 A): An agitated Continuous Stirred-Tank Reactor (CSTR) constructed from carbon steel, sized at 1.68 m in diameter, operating at a design temperature of 127.8°C and pressure of 2.43 barg.
  • Separation Column (T-101): A massive sieve tray column designed with a 6.0 m diameter and 37.2 m height to facilitate the complex entrainer-assisted reactive separation.
  • Heat Integration (E-101): A TEMA shell-and-tube heat exchanger providing a 69.7 m² heat transfer area for maximum thermal efficiency.

Collaboration & Scope

This comprehensive plant design was executed collaboratively alongside engineering peers Hemant Patel and Prasangeet. The project successfully bridges theoretical chemical engineering principles with practical, industry-standard simulation software to solve complex, multi-component separation and economic optimization challenges.
